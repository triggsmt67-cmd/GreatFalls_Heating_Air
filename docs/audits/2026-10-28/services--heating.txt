Heating symptoms worth investigating. Similar symptoms can have different causes. Testing establishes which repair is appropriate. Blower runs, but the air is cold Usually points to a failed ignition sequence. A dirty flame sensor, burned-out hot surface ignitor, or a pressure switch tripped by high wind or a frosted PVC exhaust pipe will shut down the gas burners while the fan keeps blowing. Frequent cycling (Short-cycling) If your furnace kicks on for three minutes, shuts down, and tries again, it is likely overheating. Check for a clogged air filter or blocked registers first. If the filter is clean, you may have a failing blower motor or bad limit switch. Banging, squealing, or scraping noises A mini “boom” on startup can mean delayed gas ignition—dirty burners may be letting gas build up before lighting. High-pitched squealing or metallic scraping can point to a failing inducer fan or blower wheel bearing. Stop using the furnace and arrange a professional inspection. Yellow burner flames or CO alerts Persistent yellow flames, soot, or a change in burner appearance need professional inspection; flame color alone cannot diagnose a carbon monoxide leak. If your CO alarm sounds or you suspect a leak, leave immediately and call 911 from outside. Do not stay inside to inspect or shut down the furnace.
