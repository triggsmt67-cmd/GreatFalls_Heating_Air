Can I still claim the federal $2,000 heat pump tax credit for a 2026 installation? + No. The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit under Section 25C is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Older pages advertising a heat pump credit through 2032 do not reflect that change. A qualifying 2025 installation is a separate tax-filing question; use the IRS guidance and your tax professional rather than counting a federal credit toward a new 2026 project. Sources: IRS: Section 25C credit Does my NorthWestern account qualify for the $450 heat pump rebate? + NorthWestern’s current Montana existing-home electric application lists $450 per qualifying ducted or ductless heat pump. It requires residential electric service; a NorthWestern gas account alone does not establish eligibility. The equipment must be ENERGY STAR rated or meet SEER2 of at least 15.2 and HSPF2 of at least 8.5, along with the application’s other conditions. Confirm your account and exact models before purchase; the utility makes the final eligibility decision and funding is limited. Sources: NorthWestern: current electric existing-home application Do I need approval before installing a heat pump to get a Sun River Electric rebate? + Yes. Sun River’s published air-source program says to request its rebate agreement and return it for approval before the contractor installs the system. It lists $150 per installed ton, capped at $750 per system, for buildings served by the cooperative. Payment follows installation and the co-op’s compliance inspection. Confirm the exact equipment, current efficiency requirements, and availability with Sun River before committing; its public page uses older SEER and HSPF terminology. Sources: Sun River: air-source rebate and approval steps Can I apply for Montana’s HOMES or HEEHR rebates now? + Not yet, according to Montana DEQ’s program page checked October 23, 2026. DEQ says the programs are not currently available and need final U.S. Department of Energy implementation approval before rollout. Announced funding or an estimated rebate is not an approval for your home. Do not assume a purchase made now will qualify later; wait for official application, timing, income, and equipment rules before relying on a future rebate. Sources: Montana DEQ: current Home Energy Rebates status Is NorthWestern’s up-to-$3,000 incentive for the heat pump that heats my house? + That offer is for a heat pump water heater, not the space-heating heat pump. NorthWestern’s separate program requires an existing electric resistance water-heater tank and a participating contractor; replacing a gas water heater does not qualify. Home type, age, account, and other restrictions apply, with one incentive per residence and limited funding. Confirm the current requirements and participating contractor before ordering; this page does not establish Great Falls Heating and Air’s enrollment in that program. Sources: NorthWestern: heat pump water heater program What paperwork and deadlines should I confirm before counting a rebate in my budget? + Use the application for your utility account and project, and check approval timing before buying. NorthWestern’s current existing-home electric form excludes work completed or materials purchased before August 1, 2026, and requires project completion and submission by June 30, 2027, with a recent bill and itemized receipts or invoices. Other programs have different steps, so keep model details, approvals, invoices, and a copy of the submitted application. Do not subtract an unapproved incentive from the amount you must be able to pay, and ask each administrator before assuming incentives can be combined.
